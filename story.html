Our Story

Built around a fire.

Savora began in 2009 as twelve seats around a wood oven in a former bookbinder's workshop. The fire is still the centre of everything we do.

2009

Our Beginning

Twelve seats and one oven.

The first Savora had no menu. Guests were served whatever came out of the oven that night — slow-roasted shoulders, blistered flatbreads, fruit baked in the embers. Word travelled, and within a year there was a waiting list longer than the room.

We moved to Ember Lane in 2014, keeping the original oven bricks and setting them into the new hearth. Today the dining room seats forty-two, but the idea is the same: cook simply over real fire, and give people a reason to linger.

Culinary Philosophy

“Take away everything that is not delicious, and stop.”

Fire as a tool

Oak, ember and iron give us a range of heat no stove can. We choose the flame the way a painter chooses a brush.

Time as an ingredient

Forty-day beef, seventy-two-hour dough, ten-day infusions. Patience is the one ingredient we can't buy.

Restraint on the plate

Three or four elements, each with a job. If something is only decoration, it leaves the dish.

Our Kitchen

An open hearth at the heart of the room.

Our kitchen has no doors. From any seat you can watch the oak burn down to ember, the copper pans move and every plate being finished at the pass.

Our Chef

Elena Marchetti

Raised between Bologna and the Atlantic coast, Elena trained in wood-fired kitchens across northern Spain before joining Savora as sous chef in 2012. She has led the kitchen since 2016.

Her cooking is direct and generous: perfect produce, a precise fire and the confidence to leave a plate alone. The forty-day ribeye was her first dish here; the dark chocolate entremet is her favourite.

Restaurant Atmosphere

Low light, warm oak, quiet music.

We designed the room for conversation first. Tables are spaced generously, the lamps are dimmed by hand as the evening goes on, and the cellar is always open to look around.
